# Paper B abstract candidates (P8) — claim-audited

## A. Conservative evidence-first

> Averaged Jacobian ("J-lens") operators are increasingly used to
> select causal intervention targets in language models. We show, on a
> preregistered nested fit ladder (n=120→1000, Qwen3.6-27B), that
> strong operator convergence does not license the instrument built
> from it: task-row cosines pass every gate (q50 0.9987) and all six
> aggregate scientific endpoints are fit-stable, while the selected
> sparse coordinates sit at Jaccard exactly 7/13 = 0.538462 at every
> boundary, the projector overlap stays below its floor, and a bridge-
> rescue mechanism endpoint oscillates in sign — so no canonical sparse
> lens is nominated. Selection-margin and single-prompt-influence
> falsifiers do not rescue the instrument, and a runtime-identity
> incident shows that version-level pinning does not pin backward
> semantics. On the applicability side, a target-blind mixed-precision
> calibration (frozen ceiling 0.0787) closes a five-layer Gemma-4-31B
> finite-scale tangent mismatch 17–68× above the frozen backend ceiling, and a
> two-checkpoint OLMo-3 finite-dose ladder licenses exactly one
> layer-dose cell (L56, ε=0.10, Think only). None of this bounds paired
> removal contrasts with exact dose-matched controls — an estimand
> distinction we formalize. We distill the record into a preflight and
> reporting standard for gradient-lens causal work.

## B. Protocol-forward

> When is an averaged Jacobian a causal instrument? We answer with
> validity levels: operator similarity, task-row geometry, sparse
> selection identity, and causal-endpoint invariance are separate
> licenses, and we exhibit a frozen campaign in which the first two
> converge monotonically while the last two fail — with fit-stable
> aggregate causal conclusions throughout (Q-L4). We add three
> portable boundary results: backward semantics are not pinned by
> version identity (a prospective tolerance caught a cross-era Jacobian
> discrepancy before any outcome was written); exact-JVP finite-dose
> prediction is checkpoint-, layer-, and dose-specific (one licensed
> cell across two OLMo checkpoints; a closed five-layer Gemma mismatch
> 17–68× above a calibrated backend envelope); and missing dose archives
> are a data state, not a verdict. The resulting preflight — backend
> calibration, delivery/SNR, tangent gate, fit invariance, selection
> invariance, endpoint invariance — would have caught every failure
> this campaign encountered, and each gate costs a small fraction of
> the intervention study it protects.

## C. Broad-interest, claim-safe

> Interpretability pipelines routinely fit a lens, check that it looks
> converged, and then intervene through it. We document, with fully
> reconstructible evidence, four independent ways that sequence can go
> wrong even when every convergence check passes: the sparse
> coordinates the lens selects need not be the same instrument across
> adjacent fits; a mechanism-level endpoint can flip sign while every
> aggregate stays stable; the first-order predictions the intervention
> relies on can be unlicensed at the exact layers and doses used; and
> two bit-identically-versioned runtimes can disagree materially about
> a Jacobian. Each failure was caught by a prospective, frozen gate —
> and each gate generalizes. We propose the resulting preflight as a
> minimum reporting standard for Jacobian-lens causal claims.

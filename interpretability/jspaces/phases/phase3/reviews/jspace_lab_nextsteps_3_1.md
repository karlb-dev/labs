# 0. Executive assessment

## 0.1 What Phase 2 earned

Phase 2 is no longer an exploratory curiosity. It produced a real confirmatory result package with strong research engineering:

1. The paper-faithful output-protected dynamic intervention replaced the unprotected Part 1 live ablation.
2. The capacity estimator was repaired and separately names occupancy, raw reconstruction excess, and centered variance-explained excess.
3. Statistical families were manually audited and no longer derived from filename prefixes.
4. A geometry control was built to match effective rank and removed activation energy at every item, layer, and position.
5. A binding preregistration, family-level confirmatory and replication partition, stop rules, positive controls, provenance envelopes, and an event-sourced evidence registry were used.
6. All three confirmatory model cells were completed with their own final lenses and passing swap positive controls.
7. Qwen's J-specific tail replicated on held-out families.
8. Two disjoint-corpus OLMo lenses reproduced per-item effects almost exactly.
9. The package repeatedly caught and superseded its own bugs rather than burying them.

The central result is scientifically interesting: under the repaired assay, Qwen shows a forward, paper-shaped task contrast while the OLMo 3.1 pair is one-hop dominant; exact local rank-and-energy matching does not reproduce the J arm's task damage; the protected heavy tail transfers across independent lenses; and corrected capacity differs sharply between OLMo and Qwen.

## 0.2 What Phase 2 did not yet establish

The current evidence does **not** yet justify the strongest possible manuscript sentences. In particular, the safe phrase is:

> Qwen exhibits a paper-shaped protected-ablation dissociation under the Phase 2 assay, while OLMo exhibits a different, accessibility-dominated lesion response.

Do not yet write:

> The complete global-workspace causal signature has been robustly replicated on Qwen and refuted on OLMo.

That stronger sentence remains blocked by four issues:

1. The confirmatory two-hop contrast contains only 9 items from 2 families. The held-out HP1 interaction is consequently inconclusive.
2. Output protection excludes protected **token IDs**, but the selected J span can still overlap geometrically with protected token directions. The primary matched control is explicitly orthogonal to those protected directions. This asymmetry can manufacture a J-versus-control gap through indirect output-direction deletion.
3. The exact matched control was skipped for the prose guard, so the large Instruct prose cost is not yet compared against the actual primary control.
4. The Phase 2 primary table has not been reproduced end to end by an independent, narrative-blind clean-room run.

Phase 3 should close these four gaps before spending substantial compute on model breadth.

## 0.3 The paper-level thesis to test next

The best synthesis of the current data is not simply “Qwen has a workspace and OLMo does not.” A more discriminating hypothesis is:

> J-space lesions have different functional shapes depending on what content is currently accessible, how output-adjacent that content is, and how post-training organizes internal composition. Qwen's larger measured J-space supports a paper-shaped composed-task dependence; OLMo's thinner J-space behaves more like a vulnerable factual-access channel. In all cases, most items are unaffected and a minority heavy tail carries the causal effect.

Phase 3 must decide whether this is a genuine mechanistic story or a polished description of task-selection and intervention geometry.

## 0.4 Phase 3 outcome hierarchy

The study should aim for one of these outcomes, in descending scientific value:

**Outcome A: robust mechanism.** A thick paired two-hop bank reproduces the Qwen/OLMo contrast; span-safe output protection preserves the effect; protecting the bridge content rescues it; bridge-only lesions and counterfactual bridge swaps move behavior in predicted directions; load increases dependence; and the pattern localizes to specific post-training stages.

**Outcome B: robust model-dependent causal channel.** The thick bank and span-safe audit survive, but bridge mediation is partial or heterogeneous. The paper becomes a cross-model study of J-specific, accessibility-modulated causal channels rather than a single universal workspace mechanism.

**Outcome C: output-geometry correction.** The Phase 2 tail largely disappears when the whole protected output span is preserved rather than only protected row labels. This is still valuable: it reveals a previously unmeasured confound in output-protected J-space ablation and narrows the remaining causal result.

**Outcome D: task-sampling correction.** The thick, paired bank fails to reproduce the Phase 2 model-by-task contrast. The paper becomes a methods and dataset-design result explaining why small heterogeneous multihop batteries can produce unstable workspace claims.

**Outcome E: methods boundary.** OLMo/Qwen findings remain useful, while Gemma shows that a corpus-averaged linear transport lens has architecture-dependent applicability. This can support a separate methods section or companion note, but not a claim that Gemma lacks workspace-like computation.

# 2. Forensic findings from the latest code and results

This section is not a general wish list. These are concrete issues in the current implementation that should shape Phase 3.

## 2.1 The two-hop primary is statistically thin

The confirmatory report correctly discloses that only 9 two-hop items from 2 canonical families survived the anchor difficulty filter. The HP1 bootstrap resamples families separately within task. Thus the two-hop component of the interaction is generated from only two independent clusters, even though the combined analysis reports 24 families across tasks.

No resampling technique can create missing independent two-hop families. A percentile cluster bootstrap with two clusters can yield a numerically narrow interval when those two family means happen to align, but it cannot provide robust population inference over relation families. The preregistered Phase 2 result remains part of the historical record, but the paper should treat the HP1 rejection as a **thin-cluster confirmatory result requiring thick independent replication**.

### Required action

Create a new Phase 3 two-hop bank and a new preregistered primary interaction. Do not pool the new items with the old 9-item confirmatory leg for the new primary test. Report the old result as the motivating confirmatory study and the new bank as an independent extension/replication.

## 2.2 `family_split_v1` records a seed that is not used

`partition.py` creates `rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)` but the only shuffle is guarded by `if False`. Assignment is therefore deterministic by family size and family-name ordering, not by seed 4242. This does not contaminate the frozen Phase 2 partition, because no outcome data entered the split. It does mean the report should not imply that the assignment itself was randomized by seed.

The splitter also balances difficulty and answer length but not task type. This is the immediate reason a formally balanced 32/32 family split could still leave the two-hop task nearly empty.

### Required action

1. Do not alter `d5-partition-freeze-v1`.
2. Add a registry/report clarification that the Phase 2 split was deterministic, with seed recorded but not active in tie-breaking.
3. Implement `family_split_v2` for Phase 3 with real seeded tie-breaking and explicit balancing of task variant, relation group, difficulty, answer length, and model-capability coverage.
4. Add a test proving that different seeds can change tied assignments while preserving all invariants.

## 2.3 Label protection is not span protection

The paper protocol and `protected_dynamic_v2` protect token labels by removing clean top-k token IDs from the candidate selection set. That guarantees a protected row is never selected directly. It does **not** guarantee that the span of the remaining selected J rows is orthogonal to the protected token rows.

Because the J dictionary is a coherent, nonorthogonal frame, projecting out unprotected rows may still remove a substantial component along protected answer/output directions. The current primary matched control goes further: it constructs a random span explicitly orthogonal to all protected token rows. Therefore the two arms differ in an important way:

- J arm: label-protected, but may geometrically overlap the protected span.
- matched control: label-protected **and span-orthogonal**.

A J-versus-control tail could therefore reflect indirect deletion of protected output geometry rather than deletion of a distinct internal bridge or workspace representation.

This is the most important newly reopened methods question.

### Required action

Add a protected-span audit and two new arms:

1. `meanJ_label_protected`: current paper-faithful arm, retained unchanged.
2. `meanJ_span_safe`: after label selection, remove each selected row's component inside the numerical span of the protected rows, then build the rank-safe basis from the residualized rows.
3. `matched_control_overlap_matched`: optional but strongly preferred. Match effective rank, removed energy, and the J arm's overlap with the protected span, while randomizing the remaining orientation.

Record, per layer and position:

- principal angles between the selected J span and protected span;
- projector overlap `trace(P_J P_protected)`;
- survival of each protected row under the projector;
- survival of the answer token direction specifically;
- change in clean answer logits attributable to the prompt-only intervention;
- rank and energy after residualization;
- fraction of selected rows that become numerically null after span removal.

The Phase 3 mechanism claim is permitted only if the J-specific effect survives `meanJ_span_safe` or an overlap-matched control.

## 2.4 The matched control uses raw QR on potentially dependent protected rows

`build_matched_subspace` uses QR on `[h; protected_rows]` and separately on `protected_rows`. Clean top-k token directions can be highly coherent or nearly dependent. Reduced QR on a rank-deficient matrix may include numerically arbitrary completion columns, causing the control to remove or avoid dimensions that are not actually in the protected span.

### Required action

Replace both QR calls with the package's rank-revealing SVD basis helper. Report protected effective rank. Add synthetic tests with exact duplicate, near-duplicate, collinear, and high-coherence protected rows. Re-run the mechanical dev gate after this change. Do not reinterpret Phase 2 outcomes; register this as a Phase 3 control refinement.

## 2.5 The primary matched control was skipped for prose

The confirmatory runner explicitly skips `matched_control` in the prose guard because the control consumes the J arm's per-position profile. That profile can be computed for prose exactly as it is for task items. Consequently, current statements comparing Instruct's +0.72 NLL cost against “controls” do not include the primary exact dose control.

### Required action

Run `meanJ_label_protected`, `meanJ_span_safe`, exact matched control, mechanics random, logit protected, and baseline over a broad held-out prose suite. Include NLL, top-1 agreement, KL divergence, entropy change, grammaticality preference, and sampled-generation coherence. This workstream is mandatory before claiming that Instruct's factual damage is targeted rather than diffuse.

## 2.6 The primary p-values need an independent inferential cross-check

`confirmatory_analysis.py` computes p-values from the sign fraction of percentile bootstrap replicates. Bootstrap confidence intervals are useful, but a bootstrap distribution centered at the observed statistic is not automatically a null distribution. The MixedLM cross-check groups only by family and cannot represent the shared item pairing requested by the preregistration.

### Required action

For Phase 3, use inference that respects the design:

- primary continuous interaction: compute the paired item-level direct-vs-composed contrast within model, then the Think-vs-Instruct contrast within fact, aggregate to families, and use a family-level sign-flip/randomization test or wild cluster bootstrap;
- primary J-vs-control tail: swap J/control labels within item under the sharp null, aggregate family-weighted differences, and randomize at the correct paired unit;
- estimate a crossed hierarchical model as a secondary check, with relation-family and fact/item effects;
- always show family-level dots and leave-one-family-out influence.

For Phase 2, do not retroactively replace the preregistered analysis. Add a sensitivity appendix and state that the thick Phase 3 replication is the decisive inferential check.

## 2.7 Alias aggregation and tokenization need a formal audit

The logsumexp-over-aliases endpoint is reasonable only when aliases denote distinct token sequences and are not prefix-overlapping ways of counting the same event. The BOS amendment also revealed how easily two tokenization conventions can coexist undetected. Five trailing-whitespace items still carry a double-space artifact.

### Required action

Create one typed `ScoringSpec` and one tokenizer wrapper used by capability scoring, intervention scoring, generation grading, and audit scripts. It must freeze:

- BOS behavior;
- piecewise prompt/answer concatenation;
- whitespace normalization;
- alias token IDs;
- prefix-overlap rules;
- canonical and accepted surface forms;
- truncation behavior;
- scoring-position indices.

Reject or separately classify alias sets in which one token sequence is a prefix of another. Remove trailing-whitespace artifacts from new Phase 3 primary data before freeze. Keep Phase 2 rows unchanged and flag them as a sensitivity.

## 2.8 N8 is not yet a full clean-room reproduction

`repro_v2.py` is a large improvement: it resolves immutable registry events, checks out recorded commits, verifies inputs, creates an isolated environment, and verifies payload hashes. Its explicit acceptance scope, however, is two items: one CPU result and the SmolLM golden. The primary 32B table has not been independently regenerated.

Several producers also hard-code the Drive run root, making isolated reproduction prone to reading or overwriting existing artifacts.

### Required action

Implement path-indirected reproduction using `JSPACE_RUN_ROOT`, `JSPACE_ARTIFACT_CACHE`, and `JSPACE_MODEL_CACHE`, or equivalent CLI parameters. A clean-room run must write to a new directory and compare against declared tolerances without touching the original. The N8 protocol is specified in Section 11.

## 2.9 The current living report is an audit trail, not the paper

Keeping superseded sections visible was appropriate during the campaign. It is not appropriate for the final manuscript. The current report and handout contain old capacity wording, old ladder labels, old Gemma explanations, and superseded figures below newer corrections. Reviewers should not need to solve a document archaeology puzzle.

### Required action

Preserve `REPORT_PART2.md` as the campaign log. Create a new `paper/` tree whose main text is generated only from live evidence registry items. Put the correction history in a compact “audit trail and deviations” appendix.

# 3. Phase 3 scientific questions and hypotheses

Phase 3 should have a fresh preregistration. It may use Phase 2 data to choose questions, arms, margins, and sample sizes, but its primary items and outcomes must be new.

## P3-H1: thick task-contrast replication

> With direct and composed prompts matched on underlying fact, answer, surface family, and baseline accessibility, Qwen shows a more negative composed-minus-direct J-lesion contrast than the OLMo 3.1 pair. The OLMo pair shows an accessibility-dominated pattern rather than a universal two-hop penalty.

Primary endpoint: within-fact difference in full-answer-sequence logprob delta, then model difference of that within-fact contrast.

Primary models: OLMo 3.1 Think, OLMo 3.1 Instruct, Qwen 3.6 27B.

This replaces the heterogeneous “one-hop battery versus released two-hop battery” comparison with a controlled composition contrast.

## P3-H2: protected-span leakage

> The J-specific tail survives preservation of the full clean-output protected span, not merely exclusion of protected token labels.

Primary comparison: `meanJ_span_safe` versus its exact geometry control. The current label-protected arm is retained as a paper-replication arm and compared directly against span-safe protection.

Falsifier: if the label-protected tail collapses under span-safe projection, a substantial portion of Phase 2's effect was indirect output-direction lesioning.

## P3-H3: bridge mediation

> On composed items, damage is mediated by deleting the bridge representation that the answer depends on.

Directional predictions:

1. protecting bridge-token rows in addition to output rows reduces the J lesion;
2. bridge-only ablation harms composed more than direct variants;
3. replacing the bridge with a counterfactual bridge shifts probability toward the counterfactual answer;
4. distractor-bridge lesions do not reproduce the effect;
5. these effects are strongest when clean answer confidence is low but the bridge is strongly selected.

## P3-H4: output lock-in and accessibility

> Vulnerability is controlled by whether the answer is already output-locked, not by “one hop” or “two hops” as labels.

Operational predictors:

- clean answer rank and margin;
- full-sequence baseline logprob;
- answer-direction occupancy in the selected span;
- bridge readout strength;
- distance from final token position;
- answer frequency and token length.

Use continuous predictors. Terciles are plots, not the primary model.

## P3-H5: post-training localization within OLMo

> The lesion shape changes at identifiable post-training stages rather than monotonically with “more instruction tuning” or “more thinking.”

The matched OLMo lineage is the main mechanistic laboratory because it controls pretraining far better than cross-family comparisons.

## P3-H6: working-set load engagement

> Dependence on J-space increases with controlled working-set load and compositional depth after baseline capability is matched.

This is the strongest test of whether the causal channel functions as workspace rather than factual recall plumbing.

## P3-H7: inference-time externalization

> Under the same Qwen checkpoint, official thinking-on versus thinking-off changes where and when the J-space carries bridge/task content. Any causal contrast must be measured with generation endpoints, not a teacher-forced bare answer after an open thinking register.

## P3-H8: capacity as moderator

> Across a sufficiently broad, assay-valid checkpoint panel, centered J-space capacity predicts the composed-minus-direct lesion contrast after accounting for model family and scale.

This remains descriptive until at least six assay-valid checkpoints or size points are measured. Three confirmatory models are not enough for a credible moderator regression.

## P3-H9: Gemma transport boundary

> Gemma's failure arises from a specific mismatch among global averaging, hook support, finite intervention scale, and downstream architecture. A prompt-specific infinitesimal tangent may still be valid even when a single corpus-averaged finite-scale map is not.

This is a bounded methods hypothesis, not a workspace hypothesis.

# 5. Workstream A: build the thick paired composition bank

This is the highest-leverage scientific workstream and should start first.

## 5.1 Why the current bank cannot simply be enlarged by “more hard facts”

The Phase 2 comparison conflates several axes:

- direct versus composed retrieval;
- easy canonical facts versus obscure facts;
- answer-token distribution;
- relation family;
- prompt surface form;
- model capability;
- bridge visibility;
- answer accessibility.

A larger version of the same heterogeneous battery would provide more precision without cleaner identification. Phase 3 needs matched variants of the same underlying item.

## 5.2 Three-bank design

Build three complementary banks.

### Bank F: factual relation composition

Each underlying fact bundle creates at least three variants with the **same final answer**:

1. `direct`: asks for the final relation directly from the bridge entity;
2. `composed`: gives or describes the source entity and requires source→bridge→answer;
3. `bridge_supplied`: same composed prompt, but explicitly supplies the bridge entity.

Optional fourth variant:

4. `three_hop`: source→bridge1→bridge2→answer.

Example schema, not a final item:

```json
{
  "fact_id": "river_mouth_language:amazon",
  "canonical_family": "waterway_endpoint_to_language",
  "relation_group": "geographic_composition",
  "source": "Amazon River",
  "bridge": "Brazil",
  "answer": "Portuguese",
  "direct_prompt": "The language spoken in Brazil is",
  "composed_prompt": "The language spoken in the country where the Amazon River ends is",
  "bridge_supplied_prompt": "The Amazon River ends in Brazil. The language spoken there is",
  "counterfactual_bridge": "Mexico",
  "counterfactual_answer": "Spanish",
  "accepted_answers": [" Portuguese"],
  "provenance": {...}
}
```

Use a frozen knowledge-graph or curated-source snapshot. Store source triples and validation references. An LLM may propose candidates but may not be the sole authority for factual truth.

### Bank S: synthetic in-context composition

Construct arbitrary entities and relations inside the prompt, so the task requires working memory rather than parametric memorization. Example families:

- two lookup tables composed in sequence;
- object→container→owner;
- city→route→terminal;
- symbol→operator→result;
- entity aliases with distractors;
- graph edges requiring path composition;
- reversible versus irreversible mappings.

Every prompt should have direct, composed, bridge-supplied, and counterfactual variants. This bank can scale to many independent templates and provides the cleanest causal mediation tests.

### Bank W: parametric working-set load

Detailed in Workstream E. Keep it separate from the primary factual composition bank so a synthetic load effect cannot silently substitute for factual multihop replication.

## 5.3 Family count and partition target

Target before freeze:

- at least **72 canonical two-hop relation families**;
- at least 4 capable fact bundles per family after G5;
- at least 36 families per confirmatory and replication side;
- at least 25 families per side in the cross-model capability intersection;
- no family or fact appears on both sides;
- no answer entity is shared across sides when feasible;
- no surface template hash is shared across sides.

The old 9-item/2-family leg demonstrates why item-count floors alone are inadequate.

## 5.4 Authoring constraints

For every item:

- answer never appears in the prompt;
- bridge appears only in `bridge_supplied`, never in the composed primary;
- counterfactual bridge and answer are valid under the same relation family;
- accepted aliases are frozen and prefix-audited;
- answer tokenization is recorded for all primary models;
- bridge tokenization is recorded for all primary models;
- prompt ends at the exact scoring boundary;
- no trailing whitespace ambiguity;
- no answer substring leakage after normalization;
- no duplicate fact or paraphrase crosses family partitions;
- relation direction is unambiguous;
- generation grading is deterministic and LLM-free for primary endpoints.

## 5.5 Capability and difficulty gates

Score all candidate variants on OLMo 3.1 Think, OLMo 3.1 Instruct, and Qwen in assay-wide BOS units.

Primary cross-model items must satisfy:

- capable generation on all three models;
- final answer in the frozen alias set;
- direct and composed variants both answerable;
- no severe truncation;
- no answer-in-prompt leakage;
- baseline logprob in a broad, preregistered support range;
- direct/composed difference not so large that task type is merely capability failure.

Do **not** require the same narrow difficulty window in each model. Use capability for inclusion and baseline logprob as a covariate. For matched-pair analyses, prefer fact bundles whose direct and composed baseline scores overlap sufficiently in all three models.

## 5.6 `family_split_v2`

Implement a deterministic, seed-active optimizer over families. Balance:

- number of families;
- number of items;
- task variants;
- relation groups;
- median baseline logprob by model;
- answer length by model;
- bridge length;
- capability-intersection counts;
- counterfactual availability.

Use actual seeded tie-breaking. Emit both the assignment and a balance report. The splitter must fail if either side has fewer than the preregistered two-hop-family floor.

Pseudo-interface:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class SplitConstraints:
    min_twohop_families_per_side: int
    min_intersection_families_per_side: int
    max_standardized_imbalance: float
    seed: int

partition = split_families_v2(family_table, constraints)
partition.assert_disjoint("canonical_family", "fact_id", "template_hash")
```

## 5.7 Primary thick-bank analysis

For each fact and model:

```text
lesion_delta_direct   = lp_J(direct)   - lp_base(direct)
lesion_delta_composed = lp_J(composed) - lp_base(composed)
composition_penalty   = lesion_delta_composed - lesion_delta_direct
```

Then compare composition penalties across models. This cancels answer identity, much of relation difficulty, and alias/tokenization effects.

Primary P3-P1 statistic:

```text
[composition_penalty_Qwen]
  - mean([composition_penalty_OLMoThink,
          composition_penalty_OLMoInstruct])
```

Also report Think-versus-Instruct directly. Use family-weighted estimates and a family sign-flip/wild-cluster test. Item-weighted and relation-group-weighted estimates are sensitivities.

## 5.8 Acceptance gate

The thick bank is ready only when:

- all family and leakage tests pass;

# 6. Workstream B: distinguish content mediation from protected-output leakage

This workstream determines what the Phase 2 J-specific tail actually means.

## 6.1 Add span-safe output protection

Implement a numerical protected basis from the clean top-k token rows:

```python
def span_safe_j_basis(selected_rows, protected_rows, tol=1e-5):
    q_prot = orthonormal_basis_from_rows(
        protected_rows,
        relative_tolerance=tol,
    ).basis
    safe_rows = selected_rows.float()
    if q_prot.shape[1]:
        safe_rows = safe_rows - (safe_rows @ q_prot) @ q_prot.T
    return orthonormal_basis_from_rows(
        safe_rows,
        relative_tolerance=tol,
    )
```

Do not refill lost rank by selecting more rows in the primary span-safe arm. Lost rank is a scientific observable: it tells us how much of the nominal J selection was geometrically inside the protected output span. Add a refill sensitivity arm only if preregistered.

## 6.2 Required development conditions

Run on a development subset of Bank F and Bank S:

| condition | purpose |
|---|---|
| baseline | clean reference |
| meanJ label-protected | exact Phase 2/paper arm |
| meanJ span-safe | preserves the full protected span |
| exact rank+energy matched random | current control, made rank-safe |
| protected-overlap-matched random | matches J overlap with protected span |
| mechanics random | unmatched-dose diagnostic |
| logit label-protected | output-aligned secondary |
| logit span-safe | tests whether the same leakage explains the logit effect |

## 6.3 Overlap-matched control

If span-safe and label-protected differ materially, build a control that matches:

- achieved rank;
- removed energy;
- overlap with protected span;
- answer-direction survival;
- phase and position;
- optionally temporal coherence, described below.

This control need not match semantic labels. It exists to distinguish “J content” from “a subspace positioned near the protected output geometry.”

## 6.4 Temporal-coherence control

The current matched random orientation is independently seeded by layer and position. J-selected spans may persist across nearby positions and layers. A persistent perturbation can affect a network differently from independently rotating noise even at identical local energy.

Add a secondary `matched_random_persistent` arm:

- draw an item-layer base random frame once;
- transport or reuse its free orientation across positions;
- adjust only the h-aligned component needed to match local energy;
- report inter-position and inter-layer principal-angle distributions;
- compare them with the J arm.

Do not call the current matched control “all geometry matched.” Call it **instantaneous rank-and-energy matched**. Phase 3 can test whether temporal coherence accounts for any residual difference.

## 6.5 Bridge mediation factorial

For composed items, add:

| arm | intervention |
|---|---|
| output protected | standard clean-output top-k protection |
| output + true bridge protected | also exclude/residualize bridge-token directions |
| output + distractor bridge protected | protect a matched wrong bridge |
| bridge-only lesion | ablate bridge directions, preserve answer/output |
| counterfactual bridge swap | remove true bridge and inject matched counterfactual bridge |
| answer-only lesion | diagnostic, verifies expected direct deletion |
| unrelated-content lesion | item-matched content control |

Bridge token sets must include all tokenizer variants and multi-token pieces. For multi-token bridges, report both any-piece and all-piece coverage.

## 6.6 Phase localization

The teacher-forced primary currently edits the entire prompt-plus-answer forward. Decompose the effect:

- prompt prefill only;
- answer-token teacher forcing only;
- both;
- last N prompt positions only;
- bridge-evidence span only for synthetic/in-context items;
- decode-only for generation endpoints.

This matters because an intervention during answer teacher forcing can affect later answer tokens without changing retrieval at the prompt boundary. The full-answer endpoint is valuable, but the paper needs to say where the lesion acts.

## 6.7 Layer and dose localization

For the winning mechanism arm, run:

- layer windows: early control, lower band, middle band, upper band, late control;
- single-layer scans at coarse intervals;
- k in `{2, 5, 10, 20}` with nested selection where possible;
- fixed removed-energy dose as a sensitivity;
- selected-rank and span-safe-rank dose reported separately.

The goal is not a large heatmap for decoration. The goal is to determine whether the effect is localized to a workspace-like band or accumulates from deleting content at every depth.

## 6.8 Mechanistic predictions and decision rules

### Strong bridge mediation

Promote the bridge-channel claim if all are true on new holdout families:

1. true bridge protection rescues more than distractor protection;
2. bridge-only lesion selectively harms composed variants;
3. counterfactual bridge swap increases the counterfactual answer relative to the original;
4. span-safe J retains the effect;
5. effects localize to a coherent layer/position band;
6. exact matched controls remain near zero.

### Output-leakage account

Promote this account if:

- label-protected damage shrinks sharply under span-safe protection;
- damage is predicted by J-protected-span overlap and answer-direction loss;
- overlap-matched random reproduces the tail;
- bridge protection adds little after span safety.

### Generic low-confidence account

Promote this account if:

- damage is fully explained by baseline confidence and answer rank;
- bridge/distractor arms do not separate;
- direct and composed variants behave alike after matching accessibility;
- J and logit span-safe arms converge.

# 9. Workstream E: controlled working-set and load study

The original paper's “workspace” interpretation should ultimately survive tasks that genuinely require temporary binding and composition, not only parametric facts.

# 12. Workstream H: Gemma, bounded transport autopsy

## 12.1 Do not give up, and do not keep doing the same experiment

Gemma is not a failed model cell. It is a methods anomaly with potential standalone value. However, it should no longer consume open-ended causal-grid compute. Run a bounded autopsy with explicit exit criteria.

The correct current statement is:

> For Gemma-4-31B at the tested hooks, contexts, and finite intervention scales, a single corpus-averaged residual-to-residual Jacobian is not a faithful transport model through the paper's relative layer band, and its J-lens readout does not improve on the logit lens.

Do **not** state that Gemma has no Jacobian. A differentiable network has a local derivative almost everywhere. Do **not** state that logit softcapping contaminates the fitted residual-to-residual Jacobian. In the reference implementation, the fit target is a residual layer; final norm, LM head, and softcap enter only during unembedding/readout. A “pre-cap Jacobian target” is therefore the wrong category. Legitimate target variants are final residual versus penultimate residual, plus alternative hook points.

## 12.2 Four candidate explanations

1. **Global-average cancellation:** prompt-specific Jacobians differ strongly, so their mean is a poor local tool.
2. **Finite-scale secant nonlinearity:** the infinitesimal tangent is valid, but interventions at 2–20% residual norm leave the local regime.
3. **Support-pattern sensitivity:** single-position, local-window, and uniform perturbations interact differently with sliding/global attention.
4. **Hook/target mismatch:** the residual block output and final-layer target are not the most semantically stable coordinates for this architecture.

## 12.3 Exact tangent versus finite difference

On a small number of prompts, layers, positions, and directions:

- compute an exact local JVP or VJP-derived directional prediction at the base point;
- compare with symmetric finite differences over an epsilon ladder;
- measure directional cosine, norm ratio, and second-order remainder;
- use input-fidelity and response-SNR gates;
- run the identical code on OLMo and Qwen as positive controls.

Use directions from:

- random isotropic vectors;
- selected J rows;
- logit rows;
- bridge/answer rows;
- high-variance PCs;
- attention-write and MLP-write directions.

If exact tangent prediction is good but finite differences fail rapidly, the issue is intervention scale. If the tangent itself is unstable or context-specific, averaging/hook choice dominates.

## 12.4 Prompt-specific lens test

For a small probe set, compute per-prompt Jacobians at L30/L37/L44, then compare:

- own-prompt J readout;
- another-prompt J readout;
- cluster-averaged J;
- corpus-averaged J;
- logit lens.

This directly tests whether Gemma's L22 non-identification and mid-band failure are averaging cancellation rather than absence of a usable local map.

Decision:

- own-prompt rescue, cross-prompt failure: context-specific transport;
- no own-prompt rescue: output-token coordinates are not useful at that depth or hook;
- late-only rescue: architecture delays output alignment;
- penultimate target rescue: final block specialization/noise;
- no rescue anywhere: strong boundary for this lens family.

## 12.5 Localize where nonlinearity enters

For source layers L22/L30/L37, measure response after each downstream block rather than only at the final target. Build a layer-by-layer breakdown:

```text
source perturbation -> next block -> ... -> final residual
```

For every transition, label the destination block as sliding-window or global attention. Where possible, decompose:

- attention sublayer output;
- MLP sublayer output;
- residual addition;
- normalization;
- attention-pattern KL;
- MLP gate/sign changes.

The scientific question is whether the failure is concentrated at global-attention “routing” blocks, accumulates gradually, or appears immediately.

## 12.6 Support-pattern sweep

Compare perturbations applied to:

- one source position;
- a local attention window;
- all valid positions;
- positions sharing the same token type;
- prompt-final positions only.

Gemma's 5:1 sliding/full attention pattern makes this especially informative. Uniform perturbation can partially cancel attention effects that a single-position intervention exposes, while a local window may align with architecture.

## 12.7 Target and hook sweep

Legitimate target variants:

- penultimate residual layer;
- final residual layer;
- residual immediately before the final global-attention block;
- residual after the last semantically broad block.

Legitimate readout variants:

- final norm + LM head, pre-softcap logits;
- final norm + LM head, post-softcap logits;
- token-gradient directions at the actual base residual;
- plain logit lens.

Separate fit target from readout transform in naming.

## 12.8 Smaller same-architecture checkpoint

If a smaller Gemma-4 checkpoint with the same attention pattern is available, use it to implement fp32 or higher-precision tangent tests. The goal is mechanism localization, not another headline model point.

## 12.9 Hard stop

Gemma receives at most two focused GPU blocks after the Phase 3 core:

- Block 1: exact tangent, prompt-specific J, target sweep.
- Block 2: downstream localization only if Block 1 identifies a sharp ambiguity.

Stop after Block 1 if:

- no own-prompt or penultimate-target rescue appears;
- OLMo/Qwen controls pass;
- the failure localizes clearly to finite-scale/context dependence;
- further work requires inventing a new nonlinear lens.

At that point, write the methods result. Do not pour compute into a fifth version of the same full averaged fit.

# 14. Statistical plan for Phase 3

## 14.1 Use paired contrasts before hierarchical complexity

The new bank is explicitly paired. Exploit that structure.

For each fact, model, and condition:

```text
J_effect_variant = lp_J_variant - lp_baseline_variant
C_effect_variant = lp_control_variant - lp_baseline_variant
specific_effect   = J_effect_variant - C_effect_variant
```

For task composition:

```text
within_fact_composition = specific_effect_composed - specific_effect_direct
```

For model contrast:

```text
within_fact_model_diff = within_fact_composition_modelA
                       - within_fact_composition_modelB
```

Then aggregate to canonical relation families. This avoids asking a fragile mixed model to discover the pairing after the fact.

## 14.2 Primary randomization tests

### Continuous interaction

At the family level, sign-flip the paired model-difference statistic under the null of zero symmetric effect. With enough families, use exact enumeration or at least 100,000 Monte Carlo flips.

### J-versus-control tail

Within item, exchange J and control labels under the sharp null, recompute family-weighted tail differences, and randomize. Preserve threshold, protected-answer stratum, and family weights.

### Bridge rescue

Within item, exchange true-bridge-protected and distractor-bridge-protected labels.

## 14.3 Wild cluster bootstrap

Use a wild cluster bootstrap-t as a small-cluster robustness check, especially when family effects are heterogeneous. Report it beside the randomization result, not as a replacement for more families.

## 14.4 Hierarchical model secondary

Use a model that can represent crossed fact and family structure. Options:

```text
delta ~ model * variant * condition
      + baseline_lp + answer_tokens
      + (1 + variant | canonical_family)
      + (1 | fact_id)
```

A Bayesian hierarchical model is acceptable if priors and diagnostics are preregistered. `statsmodels.MixedLM` with only one grouping factor is not sufficient as the sole cross-check.

## 14.5 Heavy-tail reporting

Always report:

- mean and median paired delta;
- tail rate at frozen thresholds;
- magnitude conditional on tail;
- ECDF or violin/raincloud plot;
- family-level distribution;
- top influential families;
- leave-one-family-out estimates;
- continuous threshold curve.

The tail is a feature of the data-generating process, not a nuisance to be averaged away.

## 14.6 Equivalence

For any null or “spared” claim:

- freeze SESOI;
- use TOST with 90% interval;
- report both one-sided p-values;
- require adequate power for equivalence;
- otherwise say “inconclusive,” not “no effect.”

## 14.7 Multiplicity

Use small, separate families:

- Family A: thick task contrast, span-safe specificity, bridge rescue;
- Family B: load interactions;
- Family C: Qwen thinking-mode factorial;
- Gemma methods tests are a separate exploratory/methods family unless independently preregistered.

Do not BH-correct a giant model×layer×dose grid and call every surviving cell a hypothesis.

## 14.8 Power design

Simulate from **family-level paired contrasts** in the new development bank, not from the defective Phase 2 prefix families. Include:

- zero-inflation;
- family ICC;
- tail prevalence;
- varying items per family;
- cross-model item correlation;
- direct/composed pairing;
- anticipated attrition from G5.

Freeze a family floor, not only an item floor.

# 17. Decision tree after each major result

## 17.1 After the span-safe audit

### Effect survives span safety

Proceed to bridge mediation and thick bank. The Phase 2 content-specific reading strengthens.

### Effect shrinks but remains

Quantify the decomposition:

```text
label-protected effect
  = protected-span leakage component
  + span-safe internal-content component
```

Carry both into the paper. Do not collapse them into one number.

### Effect disappears

Reframe Phase 2 HP3 as an output-protection geometry result. Continue the thick bank under span-safe arms; the scientific story becomes a methods correction plus any remaining Qwen task contrast.

## 17.2 After the thick bank

### Qwen forward contrast and OLMo accessibility pattern replicate

Promote model-dependent organization as the paper's central empirical result. Proceed to lineage, load, and Qwen mode.

### All models show the same paired composition effect

The Phase 2 cross-model contrast was task-sampling driven. Focus on the universal J-specific channel and bridge mediation.

### No model shows a composition effect

The paper-shaped Qwen Phase 2 result was not robust to controlled sampling. Focus on accessibility, output geometry, and methods.

### OLMo becomes forward under matched items

The old one-hop dominance was accessibility/task-mix confounding. Reinterpret post-training only after lineage data.

## 17.3 After bridge mediation

### True bridge rescue and counterfactual swaps succeed

This is the strongest mechanism result. Prioritize layer/phase localization and write the causal chain carefully.

### Bridge selected but rescue fails

Bridge readout is correlational or redundant. Test alternative intermediates, distributed multi-token bridges, and state-level rather than token-row representations.

### Distractor protection rescues equally

The rescue is dose/geometry, not bridge identity. Return to controls.

## 17.4 After Instruct specificity

### Task damage remains selective

The one-hop vulnerability is a real content-channel effect and can anchor OLMo lineage analysis.

### Prose-matched damage explains it

Describe Instruct as globally fragile under J lesions. Do not use it as evidence of factual channel specialization.

## 17.5 After load study

### J-specific effect grows with load

The workspace interpretation gains functional support.

### No load interaction, factual tail persists

Use “retrieved-content channel,” not “working memory workspace.”

### Control also grows with load

High-load tasks are generally fragile. The result does not support J specificity.

## 17.6 After Qwen mode factorial

### Thinking externalizes and reduces internal dependence

Support an externalization mechanism.

### Thinking increases J dependence during rationale

The chain of thought may itself be maintained by J-space.

### Filler equals real thinking

Extra compute, not semantic externalization, explains the difference.

## 17.7 After Gemma block 1

### Own-prompt tangent rescues

Write a context-specific transport result and stop full averaged-lens claims on Gemma.

### Penultimate target rescues

The final block is the mismatch; propose an architecture-adapted lens.

### Neither rescues, controls pass

Publish the bounded methods boundary and stop.

Phase 2 succeeded. Freeze it, reproduce it independently, and stop sanding it forever.

1. **a thick, within-fact direct-versus-composed bank** that fixes the two-family bottleneck;

> output protection separates report from computation; exact dose controls isolate direction content; controlled task pairs reveal model-dependent routing; bridge and accessibility analyses explain the heavy tail; and architecture tests define where an averaged Jacobian lens is, and is not, a valid causal instrument.

# Paper A abstract candidates (P8) — each claim-audited against the ledger

## A. Conservative evidence-first

> A 2026 interpretability result proposes that the verbalizable subspace
> read out by a fitted Jacobian lens is causally privileged in language
> models. We report a preregistered replication program on open-weights
> models (OLMo-3.1-32B Think/Instruct; Qwen3.6-27B) built around an
> instrument repair: the paper-faithful protection contract protects
> output labels, not output geometry, and 28–42% of removed activation
> energy lay inside the protected span. Under a span-safe assay with an
> exact per-site rank-and-energy-matched control, a content-specific
> heavy tail of causal damage survives on Qwen at confirmatory tier
> (tail excess +0.096, p≈1e-5) and replicates on held-out families
> (+0.102). Protecting the true intermediate "bridge" entity rescues
> composed answers (+0.43 nats over a frozen chosen distractor,
> p_holm=0.018, unreplicated by protocol); substituting counterfactual
> bridge content at matched dose is far worse than deletion (−4.05,
> development tier), though not yet separable from a direct
> answer-direction route. The OLMo pair shows a different organization:
> damage tracks answer accessibility, and across the released lineage,
> measured sparse capacity is conserved while the mapped dictionary and
> causal recruitment reorganize at the first Think transition
> (development tier; stage attribution capability-gated). No model
> earns workspace wording — the supportable noun is a knowledge-access
> channel. All primary quantities reconstruct from released data;
> instrument-validity boundaries are quantified in a companion paper.

## B. Methods-forward

> Whether a readable representation is causally load-bearing depends on
> the instrument used to remove it. We repaired the output-protected
> dynamic-ablation assay of a recent global-workspace result — label
> protection is not span protection; 28–42% of removed energy sat in
> the protected span — and re-established its core on Qwen3.6-27B at
> confirmatory tier against an exact rank-and-energy-matched control
> (+0.096 tail excess, replicated at +0.102), then extended it to
> bridge mediation and to the OLMo-3 training lineage. What survives
> repair is narrower and sharper than the original claim: a
> content-specific, bridge-consumable knowledge-access channel on Qwen;
> an accessibility-organized channel response and a training-associated
> reorganization of conserved capacity on OLMo; and no licensed
> workspace wording anywhere. Every number regenerates from registered
> data; every claim carries its evidence tier.

## C. Broad-interest, claim-safe

> Can you delete what a language model is "thinking about" without
> deleting its ability to speak? Using open ~30B models and a repaired
> version of a recent causal-ablation protocol, we show that removing
> the content of a small verbalizable subspace — while exactly matching
> the removal's rank and energy with a random control — selectively
> destroys a minority of factual behaviors on Qwen3.6-27B, replicates
> on held-out task families, and routes through readable intermediate
> "bridge" entities. The same instrument shows a different organization
> on OLMo: damage follows how accessible an answer already was, and
> across OLMo's released training lineage the channel's use, not its
> size, changes at the first reasoning-trained checkpoint (development
> tier). The results support causally load-bearing knowledge-access
> channels — not yet a global workspace — and come with a fully
> reconstructible evidence trail, including the boundaries where our
> instruments stop being licensed.
